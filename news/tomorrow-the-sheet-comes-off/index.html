Letters from Sunworks · August 30, 2026

Tomorrow.

Doors at 10 · Good Conversations at 2 · The sheet comes off at 3

Wow, I have been hanging this show all day! It truly is one of my favourite shows. Every wall in the White Gallery is doing something magical. I got a tingling feeling and teary-eyed admiring some of the new work.

It all starts Sunday morning — our shop and the White Gallery open at 10 a.m. The whole show is yours to wander from then, except for three of Dave More's new pieces, which stay hidden under a queen-size bed sheet, keeping their secret together, until 3 p.m. when Dave and I pull it down.

At 2 p.m. the chairs will be out, people will be seated, and we film Episode 8 of Good Conversations with Paul. Dave and I sit down in front of his work and go where this series goes — belonging, becoming, and what shapes a life when you pay attention to relational influences. Please be seated by 1:53; the doors shut and the camera rolls at two, and there's no late seating. Free, as always. We'll chat for 45 minutes, then take a few minutes of questions from the audience.

Then at three, the sheet comes off.

A little of their history, in Dave's own words. The three are Twilight at Parlee, New Brunswick (2020), Last Sail at Parlee (2021), and Passage — Bay of Chaleur, which he finished only this past spring; parts of its surface were still soft in August. All three are New Brunswick seascapes, and they share one instant — “that instance when the sun gives its final flash of golden light to end the day and leaves the observer suspended between the glorious conclusion of a beautiful moment and the lament at its closing.”

The first two began on a September evening in 2014, when Dave and Yvette climbed the dunes above Parlee Beach to watch the sun settle. The sea took on a mantle of jade; the retreating tide left pools where the shadows glowed crimson and orange. There was only time for a photograph. The next day he painted two small acrylic sketches, “fearful of losing the emotive power of what we had seen” — and then worked on the paintings themselves over several years. He rarely offers those studies alongside the finished work. This time he is; he feels they belong together. You can see the studies beginning at 10 a.m.

And of Passage he asks a question and then refuses to answer it. A dark sky hangs above the horizon, the foreground grasses bend in an offshore breeze, the small waves suggest a calming. “The storm may have passed. But has it? The viewer decides.”

Dave's work is not the only work in the room. Jeri Ing has brought an entirely new collection as well. Her new work is genuinely beautiful — local scenes in oil, plein air landscapes and garden paintings, and I think this is the strongest group she's brought us. Jeri painted in Red Deer for twenty-two years, ran Gallery IS downtown and turned it into her own studio, before moving to Calgary in 2021, BUT... she's back in Red Deer now.

“To be still, to stop, to look, to listen, to feel the place. To try and capture the moment — how it feels, how it smells, how the light dances, and how it all quickens your breath. To capture and interpret everything around you.

That is why I paint plein air. It’s like seeing a shooting star — magical, brief, and unforgettable.”— Jeri Ing

And new in the White Gallery, Jessie Emilie is having her first showing with us. She is an emerging artist based in Calgary, and holds a Master of Fine Arts from UBC Okanagan, where her thesis show was a series of large-scale portraits confronting how religious teaching shapes women's bodies. The collection here is small and pensive. She is exploring femininity, sexuality, and embodiment through a deeply personal perspective.

First shows matter. Come and be part of her first yes here.

A word about why I do these Good Conversations. I believe that our identities are strongly influcened by our the relational influences in our lives. — with people, with places, with ideas/beliefs, with the things and the activities we return to. A conversation is where that becomes visible: two people paying attention, and something new between them becomes possible. I don't interview anybody, only the people that I would welcome to be changed by. I sit down with those I trust and find compelling, and I follow what shows up. Every conversation changes the future in some way — that line sits at the bottom of every email I send, and I mean it with my whole heart.

And if you can't be seating by 2:00 p.m., every conversation so far is up on my YouTube channel, free to watch whenever you like — youtube.com/@reflexivepractice. Seven of them now:

Episode 1 — Erin Peden Episode 2 — Steven Collicutt Episode 3 — Margaret Hall Episode 4 — Matthew Whitfield Episode 5 — Katherine Koller Episode 6 — Malcolm Anderson Episode 7 — Dawn Candy

If one of them is worth your time, a like and a subscribe genuinely helps. The channel is small — fifteen subscribers against the seven thousand of you reading this — and YouTube only shows these conversations to new people when someone actually presses those buttons. It costs you a second and it puts a good conversation in front of a stranger.

Tomorrow, at a glance:

10:00 a.m. — Doors open. The whole show on view, Dave's three new pieces under the sheet. 1:53 p.m. — Be seated. The doors shut at two. 2:00 p.m. — Good Conversations with Paul, Episode 8: Dave More. 3:00 p.m. — The unveiling.

Come and see what we've been building. I'll be there all day. Pricing is available, and there are various options to purchase. So looking forward to seeing you tomorrow.

As ever Paul and the team of Sunworkers.
