White Gallery artist

Jessie Emilie

Oil paintings of the figure, everyday objects and the Canadian Rockies.

About the artist

Jessie Emilie is an emerging artist based in Calgary, Alberta. She holds a Bachelor of Fine Arts from the Alberta University of the Arts (2022) and a Master of Fine Arts from the University of British Columbia (2024). Her graduate thesis examined the representation of women throughout art history, with a particular focus on self-portraiture and the nude. During her studies, she also spent two years working as a Graduate Teaching Assistant in UBC’s Department of Creative Studies. After graduating, Jessie travelled extensively throughout Europe, backpacking between hostels and visiting renowned galleries, museums, and cultural landmarks. Her travels took her through the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, Germany, France, Italy, and Spain, where she immersed herself in the region’s art, architecture, food, and wine. Since returning to Canada, she has continued to find inspiration through hiking and camping in the Canadian Rockies. Alongside her professional work as a Marketing Specialist and Graphic Designer, Jessie maintains an active studio practice and has exhibited her artwork in galleries throughout Alberta and British Columbia.

Artist statement

My paintings explore femininity, sexuality, and embodiment through a deeply personal and painterly lens. I’ve long been drawn to the role of women in art history, particularly within the traditions of self-portraiture and the nude. My early work engaged with these themes alongside my own experiences with religious trauma and body image. A formative trip through Europe’s major art institutions deepened my fascination with archetypes like the reclining nude and the femme fatale, prompting me to reimagine these figures from a female perspective. The rhythm of everyday life equally inspired me. I observed there the slow mornings with coffee, the ease of conversation, and the after-work wine. That laid-back sensuality and quiet observation of the mundane continue to inform the emotional undercurrents of my work. At the center of my practice is the human form, often nude, contemplative, passive, or caught in moments of strange intimacy. Alongside the body, I often depict odd everyday objects, coffee mugs, potted plants, sardines, and anchovies on plates. These elements anchor my compositions in a reality that feels both familiar and uncanny.

This new series is an extension of my previous work and is a language formed from lived emotional experiences. I’m never more inspired than when I am feeling deeply. I have been going through a lot of life changes recently, and some hard times. I let the emotions move through my body and through my brush. Much of the imagery in my work grows from personal experiences- things I have seen, felt, or remembered and after reconnecting with nature in the Canadian Rockies, I felt my spirit and creativity come alive again. This fueled a new direction of exploration of emotional expression through mark-making. These new oil paintings reflect an ongoing struggle between my perfectionism and my desire for emotional freedom. Part of me seeks control, safety, correctness, and protection from judgment; the other wants to move instinctively, respond honestly, and allow emotion to remain loose and unresolved. I call these works my “wobbly paintings” because the wobble makes that tension visible. The wobbliness gives me permission to exist imperfectly, paint authentically, and re-enter my practice with the freedom to express myself with paint, unapologetically.
